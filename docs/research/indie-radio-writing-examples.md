# Indie Radio Writing Examples: Voice Patterns for Music Discovery

Research conducted: December 2025

## Executive Summary

This research analyzes writing patterns, voice, and storytelling approaches from high-quality independent and community radio stations worldwide. These stations represent the gold standard for music discovery narratives—earnest, knowledgeable, non-commercial voices that connect listeners to artists through personal discovery, cultural context, and scene awareness.

Key findings include voice patterns emphasizing human curation over algorithms, personal connection over commercial hype, and cultural context over genre labels.

---

## 1. Voice Patterns: Common Approaches Across Quality Indie Radio

### The Human Curator Voice

**Core Principle**: "We're not a bunch of radio pros. We're a bunch of pro-music people." — DJ Larry Mizell Jr., KEXP

Quality indie radio writing consistently emphasizes:

- **Human curation over algorithms**: "Right now, the role of radio is more important than ever. In an age where people are bombarded with endless playlists and algorithms, radio gives you something those platforms can't – a real human voice, a sense of community, and a space for discovery."

- **Personal relationship with listeners**: "I think how we really build trust and cultivate our community is hearing (listeners) when they write in... It's us hearing them, then talking about it on the air and playing songs for them." — John Richards, KEXP

- **Spontaneity and experimentation**: WFMU's "experimentation, spontaneity and humor are among the station's most frequently noted distinguishing traits." DJs maintain "complete control over program content."

### The Earnest Non-Commercial Approach

**FIP (France)**: The commercial-free model influences voice — "music interrupted only briefly for occasional announcements about forthcoming cultural events." Their writing features "relaxed and informative presentation style" with "engaging commentary and background information about the music."

**KCRW**: Described as providing "hand-picked music programming thoughtfully curated by actual people. It encourages real music discovery and delivers a truly eclectic listening experience."

**Key distinction**: Unlike commercial radio which is "driven by corporate playlists and chart success, college and community stations prioritize discovery and unique sounds."

### Writing in First Person Without Stating the Obvious

From album review best practices for college radio writers:
- "Speak in the first person without stating 'I think' or 'In my opinion'"
- "Avoid using terms such as 'the audience,' 'listeners,' or 'fans' and not assume to know their thoughts"

This creates intimacy while avoiding presumption.

## 2. Discovery Narratives: How Stations Tell "We Found This Artist" Stories

### The Long-Form Feature Approach: KEXP's "Throwaway Style"

**Model**: Monthly column "dedicated to spotlighting the artists of the Pacific Northwest music scene through the age-old practice of longform feature writing."

**Narrative framework**: "Whether it's an influential (or overlooked) band or solo artist from the past, someone currently making waves in their community (or someone overlooked making great music under everybody's nose), or a brand new act poised to bring the scene into the future."

**2025 Feature Examples**:

- **AJ Suede (February)**: "The Royal Quest of AJ Suede" — Framed as conversation with "one of Seattle's hallmark rap artists" about "his 35th album released in the past eight years, his impressive artistic lineage, growing up in East Harlem, and the circumstances that brought him to Seattle."

- **Shaina Shepherd (July)**: "Shaina Shepherd and the Devil's Music" — "A sprawling conversation that lasted well over an hour—where they talked about growing up in the church, sneaking around to listen to alternative rock music, and her long journey from Tacoma to Brooklyn and back around to the Northwest."

- **Blood Lemon (August)**: "From Boise to the World" — "Paints a portrait of the Boise rock scene for the uninitiated and is an in-depth look at a band poised to be what columnist Martin Douglas calls 'the next significant band to come out of Boise.'"

- **Tiny Vipers (September)**: Writer "traveling down to his old stomping grounds of Tacoma to speak with Jesy Fortino... He arrived at her home with lunch and they spent the next couple hours talking about Fortino's life and careers."

**Key pattern**: Physical presence ("traveling down," "arrived at her home with lunch"), extended conversation ("well over an hour," "next couple hours"), and framing artists within geographic/scene context.

### The Themed Playlist Narrative: BBC Radio 6 Music

**"Artist in Residence"** program takes listeners on "a journey into the musical soul of an artist, based around a theme or mood."

**Phoebe Bridgers example**:
- Playlist 1: "Not Sad; Songs in which I find happiness"
- Playlist 2: "Pain; Songs for dark, depressing days" — "devoted to the darkest corner of her library of songs"

**Laurie Anderson example**: Four themed playlists:
- The real world
- Love and energy
- Loneliness and change
- The dream world

**Writing approach**: Thematic organization reveals artist's inner world and influences, creating discovery through association and curation.

### The Daily Discovery Format: KCRW's "Today's Top Tune"

**Format**: "A free song each weekday curated for your listening pleasure, that represents a cross section of KCRW's eclectic music format."

**Writing style examples**:

**Steven Bamidele**: "London-based, Nigerian singer-songwriter-producer Steven Bamidele just released his sophomore album, The CRASH!. It is a deeply personal and soulful meditation on the weight we place on relationships, the cost of growth, and our existential search for direction."

**Bullion**: "Lisbon-based Nathan Jenkins, the producer and songwriter working as Bullion, is an enduring cult fixture and has a new album called Affection. His brand of warm, shiny pop is as intoxicating as it gets on 'World_train,' for which Bullion enlists a perfect collaborator, Charlotte Adigéry, to turn the track into a folky, meandering ditty."

**Pearl & The Oysters**: "'Under The Mandarin Moon,' or as Pearl & The Oysters say 'Sous la lune mandarine,' is the latest example of the group's musical superpower: exploring a difficult subject over a wistful, Bossa-nova-tinged tune."

**S.G. Goodman**: "Born and bred in Western Kentucky, S.G. Goodman is an old soul of a storyteller whose work points to ancient traditions and lost ways of life. Her new album, Planting by the Signs, is her first in three years — and most likely her best work yet. Over eleven songs, Goodman puts forth the notion that the only way forward for any of us is together."

**Key patterns**:
- Geographic anchoring (location establishes context)
- Brief career context ("sophomore album," "enduring cult fixture," "first in three years")
- Evocative genre/style descriptors ("wistful, Bossa-nova-tinged," "warm, shiny pop")
- Thematic framing (relationships, growth, togetherness)
- Specific musical details ("Over eleven songs")

### The Unearthed Platform Model: Triple J (Australia)

**Format**: Digital platform + radio integration where artists upload music to be heard by station team and listeners.

**Scale of discovery**: "As of 2023, the platform hosts over 170,000 tracks from over 85,000 independent musicians."

**Success stories framing**: "Cited as a 'revolutionary idea' that 'arguably changed the entire Australian music landscape,' the Unearthed brand has been responsible for discovering some of Australia's most celebrated musical acts, including Flume, Missy Higgins, Vance Joy, Gang of Youths, Grinspoon and the Kid Laroi."

**Weekly structure**: "Feature artist every week and their music will get played on Triple J which is amazing national exposure."

**Podcast approach**: "Claire Mooney from the triple j Unearthed team takes you through the five best tracks of the week every Friday... which also features specials with music industry advice to deep dives on scenes from around the country."

**Key pattern**: Platform-as-discovery combined with active curation and scene education.

## 3. Scene Context: How They Connect Artists to Scenes/Movements

### Geographic Scene Anchoring

**KEXP's Regional Identity**:
- "The other most Seattle thing we do is play local music every hour. Seattle music is in the DNA of KEXP. It's why we've been around for 50 years. The artists and people who made music in our city supported us when no one else did." — John Richards

- **Throwaway Style mission**: "Celebrates the community of musicians that makes the Pacific Northwest one of a kind."

**World Cafe's "Sense of Place" Series**:
- "The World Cafe team visits musical hotspots all around the globe for the show's 'Sense of Place' series. In each city, the team captures the sights and sounds, records studio sessions with native musicians, and interviews musical influencers to present them on the air and online in a week-long feature for each city."

**Triple J's National Australian Focus**:
- "Plays more Australian content than commercial networks"
- Mission to "offer an alternative to mainstream commercial radio by showcasing a diverse array of music genres and promoting independent Australian artists"

### Scene as Living Ecosystem

**FIP's approach to transitions and flow**: "All music programming is hand-picked by a small team of curators, who create three-hour blocks of music. They abide by a few rules, most notably paying close attention to how tracks transition from one to the other, across genres and styles."

**Pattern**: Scene context emerges through juxtaposition and flow, not just through explicit commentary.

**WFMU's "uncategorizable strangeness"**: Programming includes "78 RPM Records, jazz, psychedelia, hip-hop, electronica, hand-cranked wax cylinders, punk rock, gospel, exotica, R&B, radio improvisation, cooking instructions, classic radio airchecks, found sound, dopey call-in shows, interviews with obscure radio personalities... as well as Country and western music."

**Pattern**: Scene context is deconstructed — celebrating eclecticism over genre boundaries, creating new juxtapositions that redefine musical relationships.

### Cultural Movement Framing

**BBC Radio 6 Music's positioning**: "The UK's most influential alternative music station. By supporting new and innovative artists, it plays a crucial role in shaping the future of the music industry."

**Triple J's cultural impact**:
- "Stands as a cornerstone of Australia's radio landscape, celebrated for its dedication to fresh, innovative music and support for emerging talent"
- "On any given day, hundreds of thousands of listeners across the country are tuned in. Label owners, promoters, publicists and musicians follow the station with relentless fascination, as its playlist and musical preferences can literally make, delay, or break careers in the notoriously fickle music business."

**Critical awareness**: Triple J has been "accused of promoting a homogeneous Australian music scene" with critics claiming artists need the "Triple J sound" to be played — "a sort of indie surf, slightly pop persuasion."

**Pattern**: Best indie radio is aware of its tastemaking power and the cultural responsibility that comes with it.

## 4. Personal Touch: DJ Personality in Commentary

### The Oral Tradition and Storytelling

**Historical context**: "DJs were committed to having a personal conversation with their audiences. The oral tradition of storytelling, speaking in rhythm and rhyme, speaking in an improvised style, as well as an animated delivery, is a cultural expression that was familiar to the masses—which is why so many people enjoyed personality radio."

**During the 1950s-70s**: "Radio DJs exerted considerable influence on popular music because of their ability to introduce new music to the radio audience."

**Modern shift**: "Most commercial radio stations use music playlists selected by music directors based on market research... The exception is college and independent radio DJs, who are likely to curate all of the music in their segments."

### WFMU's Individualistic Voice

**Show title examples** demonstrate extreme personal voice:

**DJ/Rupture's "Mudd Up!"**:
- "GRIME ANALOGS & THEIR DISCONTENTS"
- "THUNDER MADE FROM SILENCE"
- "IGUANA BREATH, LIZARD EARS"
- "IN PRAISE OF THE AIRHORN"
- "NOTHING LASTS FOREVER - SOMETHING ELSE ALWAYS HAPPENS"

**"This Is The Modern World" with Trouble**:
- "'When you assert that the future is already decided, you undermine the motivation to participate in shaping that future' - Rebecca Solnit"
- "'revolution is not a one time event' (audre lord)"
- "wry little stories, narrated by women too smart for their own good"

**Other shows**:
- "It's Your Turn To Wear The Demon's Skull."
- "Feed your head!"
- "The King of Luk Thung!"

**Pattern**: Poetic, literary, playful, sometimes cryptic. Each DJ develops completely individual voice. "Playlist tables are not in a consistent format, and the DJs are inconsistent in how they enter artist names."

### Morning Becomes Eclectic's Conversational Expertise

**Tagline**: "KCRW's signature music program — defying format since 1977."

**Format**: "Genre-spanning discoveries and classics from all eras, live performances, candid chats, and more."

**Voice example**: "New gems to help you kick off a new week, like the futuristic soul sounds of west London's George Riley and a City Pop-inspired song from Ginger Root."

**DJ description**: "John Tejada crafts seamless journeys through electronic music, blending deep cuts and future sounds to connect the underground's past with its evolving future."

**Pattern**: Knowledgeable but accessible, using specific genre/scene references ("City Pop," "futuristic soul") while maintaining conversational tone.

### BBC Radio 6 Music's Artist-as-Curator Voice

**Artist in Residence: AAA format**: "Filmed at the BBC's legendary Maida Vale Studios... gives musicians the opportunity to embark on a musical journey with some of the world's finest equipment and engineers. Led by the artist themselves, the short film will see the artist produce a filmed performance, collaboration or conversation – offering fans an even deeper insight into their creative process."

**CMAT on her process**: "I use the voice notes app on the iPhone more than I've ever used any instrument in my life… I've used this more than the guitar, more than the omnichord… I guess what I'm saying is that I use my voice most of the time when I am writing a song."

**Loyle Carner**: "Joined by his producer Kwes and engineer Nick Mills to break down tracks from his latest album, hugo. They dissect individual tracks, giving a powerful and personal insight into the way they were made."

**Pattern**: Technical detail meets personal revelation. Artists speak in their own voice about process, creating intimacy through specificity.

### The Healing and Community Role

**KEXP's "Music Heals" initiative**: "Connecting folks through the healing power of music has become 'a part of our everyday DNA,' adding with characteristic Seattle humor that 'Seattle's a fucking depressing city... so it's our role, in the music we play and the humanity that we always exemplify, to anchor people in optimism.'" — DJ Mizell

**Community Radio Philosophy**: "This festival is about all the different ways in which we can celebrate and discover music and different artists and radio is the perfect tool for that, it's a platform of storytelling and discovery, and stations play just as much a part of the musical ecosystem as artists, musicians and DJs."

**Pattern**: DJs position themselves as connectors and community builders, not just music programmers.

## 5. Specific Writing Patterns and Techniques

### A. Opening with Geographic/Cultural Anchors

**Pattern observed across stations**:
- "London-based, Nigerian singer-songwriter-producer..."
- "Born and bred in Western Kentucky..."
- "Lisbon-based Nathan Jenkins..."
- "West London's George Riley..."
- "Boise, ID power trio..."

**Purpose**: Immediately establishes cultural context and scene connection.

### B. Career Context in Brief

**Examples**:
- "Sophomore album"
- "Enduring cult fixture"
- "First in three years — and most likely her best work yet"
- "35th album released in the past eight years"
- "New album called Affection"

**Pattern**: Just enough context to understand artist's trajectory without overwhelming detail.

### C. Evocative Musical Descriptors

**Instead of generic genre labels**:
- "Warm, shiny pop"
- "Wistful, Bossa-nova-tinged tune"
- "Futuristic soul sounds"
- "Folky, meandering ditty"
- "City Pop-inspired"
- "Explosive rock will transcend listeners into another world"

**Pattern**: Hybrid descriptors, sensory language, specific genre-niche references that demonstrate knowledge.

### D. Thematic Framing Over Technical Description

**Examples**:
- "A deeply personal and soulful meditation on the weight we place on relationships, the cost of growth, and our existential search for direction"
- "Exploring a difficult subject over a wistful, Bossa-nova-tinged tune"
- "Points to ancient traditions and lost ways of life"
- "The notion that the only way forward for any of us is together"

**Pattern**: What the music is about (emotionally, thematically) matters as much as what it sounds like.

### E. The "Artist's Superpower" Technique

**Example**: "The latest example of the group's musical superpower: exploring a difficult subject over a wistful, Bossa-nova-tinged tune."

**Pattern**: Identifying what makes an artist distinctive, their signature move or unique capability.

### F. Conversational Scene-Setting

**Long-form feature examples**:
- "He arrived at her home with lunch and they spent the next couple hours talking..."
- "A sprawling conversation that lasted well over an hour..."
- "In the living room of the band's shared living and performance space, known as 'the Club House'..."

**Pattern**: Physical presence and temporal detail create intimacy and authenticity.

### G. The Transition Flow Technique (FIP Model)

**Approach**: "Careful attention paid to smooth and unobtrusive transition from one song to the other."

**Result**: "Listeners can hear bossa nova, jazz, cabaret, electro, funk, disco, and gems spanning the '60s to today. It's all seamlessly integrated, yet not beat-mixed, so the eclectic nature flows smoothly and organically from one track to the next."

**Rule**: "Making sure that a song is never played twice in a 48-hour window."

**Pattern**: Curation as art form — the relationship between songs matters as much as individual selections.

## 6. What to Avoid: Anti-Patterns from Commercial Radio

### The Algorithm Problem

**Critique**: "In an age where people are bombarded with endless playlists and algorithms, radio gives you something those platforms can't – a real human voice, a sense of community, and a space for discovery."

**Anti-pattern**: Relying on data-driven recommendations without human context and curation.

### The Corporate Playlist Problem

**Context**: "Most commercial radio stations use music playlists selected by music directors based on market research."

**Result**: Loss of DJ agency and tastemaking influence. "The role of the commercial DJ has shifted toward hosting themed segments of storytelling, music discussion, current events, and journalism."

**Anti-pattern**: Playlist-driven programming that removes human curation and risk-taking.

### The Genre Homogeneity Problem

**Triple J critique**: "Routinely been accused of promoting a homogeneous Australian music scene... artists needed the 'Triple J sound' to be played on the station."

**Anti-pattern**: Creating institutional bias that favors certain sounds over others, limiting diversity.

### The Schmoozing vs. Service Problem

**Best practice**: "While there may be a misconception that promoters are people who built relationships by schmoozing, the best promoters are actually 'polite, organized people who make life easy for the DJs and writers they submit to.'"

**Anti-pattern**: Relationship-building through charm rather than through quality service and respect for DJs' time.

### The Over-Explanation Problem

**From writing guidelines**: "Speak in the first person without stating 'I think' or 'In my opinion.'"

**Anti-pattern**: Hedging, over-explaining, stating the obvious, or being unnecessarily tentative.

## 7. Key Takeaways for Agent Voice Development

### Core Principles

1. **Human Curator First**: Position as a knowledgeable human making connections, not an algorithm serving predictions.

2. **Geographic & Scene Anchoring**: Always provide cultural/geographic context when introducing artists. Where are they from? What scene are they part of?

3. **Thematic Over Technical**: Lead with what music is about emotionally/thematically, not just what it sounds like sonically.

4. **Evocative Hybrid Descriptors**: Use specific genre-niche references and sensory language instead of broad genre labels.

5. **Personal Discovery Narrative**: When appropriate, frame discoveries as personal finds or scene investigations, not algorithmic matches.

6. **Flow and Transition**: Consider how recommendations connect to each other, not just individual quality.

7. **Confident First-Person Voice**: Write with authority from personal perspective without hedging ("I think," "in my opinion").

8. **Community and Connection**: Frame music discovery as part of building community and cultural understanding.

### Voice Spectrum Examples

**Most Formal** (BBC Radio 6 Music):
"Led by the artist themselves, the short film will see the artist produce a filmed performance, collaboration or conversation – offering fans an even deeper insight into their creative process."

**Conversational Knowledgeable** (KCRW):
"His brand of warm, shiny pop is as intoxicating as it gets on 'World_train,' for which Bullion enlists a perfect collaborator, Charlotte Adigéry, to turn the track into a folky, meandering ditty."

**Personal Scene-Based** (KEXP):
"He arrived at her home with lunch and they spent the next couple hours talking about Fortino's life and careers."

**Playfully Literary** (WFMU):
"THUNDER MADE FROM SILENCE"
"It's Your Turn To Wear The Demon's Skull."

**Community-Focused** (KEXP):
"Seattle music is in the DNA of KEXP. It's why we've been around for 50 years. The artists and people who made music in our city supported us when no one else did."

### For Our Agent Specifically

Given KEXP baseline + technical capabilities:

- **Use geographic/scene anchoring** from user's listening history or artist metadata
- **Frame discoveries thematically** based on listening patterns ("You've been exploring...")
- **Create flow narratives** when suggesting multiple tracks ("This leads naturally to...")
- **Use specific genre-niche descriptors** that demonstrate knowledge depth
- **Write with confident first-person voice** without algorithmic hedging
- **Connect to cultural moments/movements** when relevant
- **Acknowledge Seattle/KEXP DNA** as cultural touchstone but don't be limited by it

## Appendix: Station-Specific Characteristics

### KEXP (Seattle)
- **Strength**: Long-form artist features with scene context
- **Voice**: Conversational, scene-embedded, personal discovery
- **Format**: Throwaway Style monthly features, Music Heals initiative, local-every-hour commitment
- **Key figure**: Writer Martin Douglas (Throwaway Style columnist)

### WFMU (New Jersey)
- **Strength**: Maximum DJ autonomy and personality
- **Voice**: Playful, literary, cryptic, individualistic
- **Format**: True freeform — DJs control all programming
- **Defining trait**: "Experimentation, spontaneity and humor"

### KCRW (Santa Monica)
- **Strength**: Daily curation with evocative prose
- **Voice**: Knowledgeable tastemaker, accessible expert
- **Format**: Today's Top Tune (daily), Morning Becomes Eclectic (3-hour daily show)
- **Key trait**: "Hand-picked music programming thoughtfully curated by actual people"

### WXPN / World Cafe (Philadelphia)
- **Strength**: Artist interviews and in-studio performances
- **Voice**: Intimate conversational, craft-focused
- **Format**: Sense of Place (geographic deep dives), Culture Corner (monthly)
- **Key trait**: "Intimate conversation with an artist focusing on their craft, songwriting, and inspirations"

### The Current (Minnesota Public Radio)
- **Strength**: Broad knowledge across genres
- **Voice**: Eclectic expertise
- **Format**: Local Current service for Minnesota artists
- **Notable**: Steve Seel's "broad knowledge of many musical genres"

### BBC Radio 6 Music (UK)
- **Strength**: Artist-as-curator programs
- **Voice**: Authoritative alternative tastemaker
- **Format**: Artist in Residence, BBC Music Introducing
- **Key trait**: "The UK's most influential alternative music station"

### Triple J (Australia)
- **Strength**: Platform-based discovery at scale
- **Voice**: Youth/indie tastemaker, national scene builder
- **Format**: Unearthed (digital upload platform), Home and Hosed (nightly local music)
- **Impact**: "170,000 tracks from 85,000 independent musicians"

### FIP (France)
- **Strength**: Seamless genre transitions, flow as art
- **Voice**: Minimal, informed, focused on music flow
- **Format**: 3-hour hand-curated blocks with transition attention
- **Key trait**: "Commercial-free music interrupted only briefly for occasional announcements"
